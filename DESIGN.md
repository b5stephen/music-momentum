# Design

The palette and visual language for the app and its website. The app's colours come from the asset catalog and the website's from `docs/styles.css`; this file explains them. Exact sizes and per-screen details live in the code.

## Palette

| Name | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Coral | `#FF6640` | The accent. `AccentColor` in the asset catalog, the same sRGB `#FF6640` in light and dark, as on the website. Keep it sRGB: Display P3 components with the same numbers render a redder coral on an iPhone. |
| On-accent | `#1A0A05` | Text and glyphs on a solid coral fill. `OnAccent` in the asset catalog. |
| Accent text | `#C43A12` light, `#FF6640` dark | Coral used as text on a plain background. `AccentText` in the asset catalog. |
| Amber | `#FFB23E` | Website only. |
| Rose | `#FF4F8B` | Website only. |
| Violet | `#9C8CFF` | Website only. |
| Ground | `#0D0A0F` | Website background, a near-black plum. The app uses the system backgrounds. |
| Surface / Raised | `#17121A` / `#231B24` | Website cards and raised panels. |
| Line | `#2E2530` | Website borders and dividers. |
| Text / Muted | `#F6EFEA` / `#B9ACB4` | Website body text and secondary text. |

On the website, each feature gets its own accent: coral for speed, amber for markers, rose for loops and violet for sync. Colour comes from solid blocks and accents, never gradient washes.

## Principles

- **One tint.** Coral is the app's only accent. Amber, rose and violet stay on the website; bring one into the app only for a job the tint can't do.
- **Coral means "on" or "act on this".** The running loop, the speed you saved. Never decoration, or it stops meaning anything. Play/pause is the primary colour.
- **Only one state shouts.** Solid coral is for looping and for graphics (the speed knob's lit scale, the loop being played on the timeline). Everything else that's coral is a tint: `.tint` at 12–16% with `AccentText`. Settled things are `.quaternary` with secondary text.
- **Fill carries state; glyph carries kind.** A dot is a marker and a short bar (`SpanGlyph`) is a loop, as they're drawn in the timeline's marker lane; the fill says whether it's idle or looping. On Practice, idle pills are glass like its other buttons: grey beside the glass Change Song and Mark read as a different, disabled kind of control.
- **Neutral controls stay neutral.** Nudge, cue, skip, the volume slider and the like use system fills, not coral. Toolbar glyphs that act on the screen (Saved's add and sort, a sheet's save tick) are `AccentColor` on the toolbar's own glass.
- **Banners stay away from coral's hue.** Warnings are yellow; errors are neutral grey with a coral icon. No orange or red near the accent; they read as the same colour.
- **Give text in buttons a concrete colour.** A hierarchical style like `.secondary` resolves against the button's tint and comes out a faded coral. `.bordered` turns grey once its text is recoloured, so tinted chips are drawn by hand.

## Artwork colour

With a song loaded, Practice is painted in the cover's own colours, the way Apple Music's Now Playing is: the cover blurred into a mesh gradient, darkened, always with light text. Coral steps aside there, since it vanishes on a red or orange cover; "on" becomes a solid fill of the text colour. Covers without colours, and the screen with nothing loaded, keep the system background and coral. While a new cover is sampled, the colour MusicKit ships with it shows first; when there's none (often, for a Mac library cover), the last cover's colours stay rather than flashing the system background between two songs. How the colours are tuned is recorded in `ArtworkPalette`.

## Marking

Mark is for playing along, so it never gets in the way of the song. Pressing it saves a marker at once, a little before the playhead to allow for reaction time (about a third of a second of listening, less when the song is slow, none when it's paused), and doesn't pause, seek, open anything or touch the loop. A second press within a second is ignored. Mark and Edit… is the slower path, which pauses and opens the marker sheet; so does a long press on Mark. Undo Mark takes the point back. On iPhone and iPad a toast appears 12pt above the tab bar, over the volume row and the pills, or, on wide layouts, inside the playback panel over the pills and Mark, which are what it's about. Its header says "Marker 7 saved at 2:20.1" with Undo, and under it "Still playing · pick a name, or carry on" ("Paused" when the song isn't playing). Five guessed names follow as chips that wrap onto a second row rather than scrolling the last one away, with Edit… and Loop… as the last two chips, kept together, and the first guess the brightest, then a line that counts down its eight seconds (six ran out while still reading the guesses); a swipe down dismisses it sooner, as VoiceOver's escape does. Loop… opens New Loop over the marker's section, already looping, which Edit… then Make a loop took two taps to reach. The card is the cover's darkest tone (the palette's shadow), near-opaque, with a hairline and a shadow so the knob doesn't show through; the design board's slate was only its sample song's ground, and fixed, it sat as a blue-grey slab on every other cover. Before a cover loads it's that slate. and the chips are a flat fill and a hairline, since glass on glass vanished. The toast doesn't time out under VoiceOver, which is told instead.

The sheets, Edit Marker and Edit Loop, are two cards on the grouped background: the name with its chips (which include the current name, so an edit shows it selected), then the times, the strip, the nudges and the facts together, since they're all about one spot. The strip opens about ten seconds around a marker, or one and a half times a loop's length, and pinch zooms it; there's no zoom row. A marker's single time is plain, not tinted; a loop opens with Start selected, and the selected panel's label is in the accent. Set to playhead, the nudges and the listening buttons are capsules on system fills in one size family, with Delete bare under the cards. Both sheets end in two rows of listening controls: Edit Loop's Loop, −5, play/pause, +5, then Play from start and Play into end; Edit Marker's the same without Loop, then Play from marker and Play into marker, which leads in by a second. Make a loop is a tinted chip at the end of "Runs until…", where Edit Loop keeps Set start to playhead. Nothing on a sheet is solid coral except Loop when on.

Markers start sections that run to the next marker. Loops lie over sections without splitting them, and are made from a marker with Make a loop, by dragging on the Mac's lane, or with A·B. Make a loop keeps a marker that's already a section start, since removing it would merge the sections round it; one that isn't one yet makes way. From New Marker the marker is never saved, and from Edit Marker on the point Mark just made, still unnamed, the loop replaces it, which the sheet says under "Runs until". Undo puts the marker back.

## A·B

A·B catches a loop on the beat. The first press sets A, a reaction's width before the playhead as Mark does, and starts the song if it's paused, since B is found by listening (B never starts it: paused there, the user put it there); the second (or Loop) sets B and loops at once, back from A. On the Mac a third starts again from a new A, replacing the unsaved loop, since throwing it away alone was too easy by accident, and Discard is its own item: Esc and Markers › Discard A–B Loop. On iPhone and iPad a third tap clears the loop instead: Discard alone in the button's long-press menu left no visible way to get rid of one. The long-press menu keeps Discard, for a lone A. The A key on an iPad keyboard walks the Mac's way. Nothing is saved: the loop is a scratch one until Save Loop, which makes it "Loop N", looping in its place, and offers to name it (the toast on iPhone and iPad, the name field under its bar on the Mac). Undo puts the scratch loop back. Loop off, or looping something else, keeps it as a ghost: Loop brings it back from off, and on the Mac a click on its bar loops it again, as a click on any loop's does. Only discarding, saving or changing song clears it. A marker's menu has Start A·B Here. Edit Times… (the A·B bar's right-click menu on the Mac, a long press on the A·B button on iPhone and iPad, beside Save Loop and Discard) opens the loop in New Loop to set its ends exactly; saving there saves it, Cancel leaves it as it was.

The button reads "A·B", filled like Loop once A is set: "B" while it waits, "A·B" once the loop is made. Where Mark has its own line it's a glass capsule left of Mark, matching it, and Restart keeps its place left of play. In the compact layout, where Mark shares the clip row and there's no room, it's an outlined capsule in Restart's place left of play, so the two loop makers flank the transport, and Restart is only in the menus (⌘←); taking Restart's place everywhere lost a control that gets used a lot. On the Mac it's a glass capsule beside Mark, since both are pressed on the beat and both draw into the lane under them, and the transport keeps Restart. In the lane the growing loop is a faint bar led by A's edge on iPhone and a dashed "A" bar on the Mac; looping, it's the looping fill with a dashed edge and a ring, so it reads as not saved; with the loop off it's a dashed outline. Its ends trim like any loop's on the Mac. The caption or header says where it is ("A at 1:16 · tap B to close", "Looping A–B · 11s, not saved", "A–B loop paused · Loop resumes it"), and the save prompt under the title grows to "Save loop & 70%" when both are pending.

## Wide iPad

A landscape window with room for Saved beside the narrowest card, and tall enough for the phone layout in the card, drops the tab bar: Practice becomes a resizable card hovering over Saved, which runs the full width of the window behind it. It's one page, not a split: no divider, and the cover's colour glows on the page around the card. Saved drops what the card already says: no large title, and a minimal empty state.

Saved's rows carry no controls of their own, on the phone as well as here, as in the Mac's sidebar: a tap practises the song, the speed is quiet trailing text, and a long press holds Practice From (each marker), Change Speed…, Add Marker… and Delete. Beside the card, that leaves nothing on the page to clash with the cover's glow. Lists are the other way to organise it. On iPhone and iPad they're a row of chips above the songs, shown once there's a list: All, Not in a List while anything is, each list with its count, and a + for a new one. A chip scopes Saved to one list at a time, coral-tinted while it's on, glass beside the card like Practice's pills, and a long press holds Add Songs…, Rename… and Delete List. Inside a list a row's swipe removes it from the list, in grey since it loses nothing, rather than deleting the song. Selecting several songs is the system's own edit mode, from the toolbar's ⋯. On the Mac, Saved's header names the scope, one list at a time as on iOS, and a separate Manage Saved Songs window, the one place lists combine (all of them or any), is a table for organising in bulk, with drag onto a list in its sidebar. Saved can also be viewed as Lists instead of Songs (⋯ on iOS, the header menu on the Mac): a collapsible folder per list, then Not in a List, in place of the chips or the scope, under an All Songs folder that holds every song once and starts closed, so a big library doesn't push the lists off the screen. It hides while searching, since searching opens every folder and each match already shows under its list, with songs in the sort's order and no letter headers. A song in several lists sits in each folder, a swipe or Remove takes it out of the folder it's under, and searching opens every folder and hides the empty ones. Every open list ends with an Add Songs… row, in a folder and under a list's chip alike, which opens the list's song picker; its first row, Find in Apple Music…, saves a new song and ticks it. An empty list's row is laid out as a song, a coral-tinted tile with a plus where the artwork goes, as Music's Add Music is in an empty playlist, since adding is the one thing to do there. Under songs it's a bare grey plus in the artwork's column and small grey text at the titles' inset, in a shorter row: a tile per list, coral or grey, read as one more song and outweighed the titles. A grey label and plus both at the titles' inset floated, belonging to neither the header nor a row, and was dropped. In the ⋯ menu, Songs and Lists sit under a View As heading, since an inline picker's own label never shows in an iOS menu. The Manage window stays a flat table. From twelve songs, about a screenful, the list gets headers: Title (the default) and Artist file songs under the first letter of the title or artist, past a leading "The", "A" or "An" as Music does (The Beatles under B, The Sound of Silence under S), with Music's A–Z index down the side on iPhone and iPad, and Last Practiced under Notes' date headings (Today, Yesterday, Previous 7 Days, Previous 30 Days, this year's months, then years) with songs never played last. Whether to group is settled only when Saved appears, the sort or search changes, a song is added or the app comes back, never while a song is deleted under the user's finger, and the rows slide into their sections. A separate group-by was left out, since grouping one way while sorting another scatters every section. There's no Date Added: nothing records when a song was first saved.

Practice's save, change-song and Mark buttons and its loop pills are glass too, so they take on the cover like the knob does. Mark was bare text on the caption line until people didn't see it as a button. The save prompt is the same glass in the same colour as Change Song: tinted coral beside it, the pair read as two unrelated controls. The prompt is only there while there's a speed to save; once it's saved, the dot on the knob's scale says so. Mark, on its own line at the bottom, is the same 44pt capsule as the prompt, so glass on Practice means one thing at one size. In this layout the toast sits over the foot of the wheel's column.

## Mac

The Mac window is the iPad's wide layout turned into a window rather than a new app. The cover's mesh fills the whole window, title bar and sidebar included, so loading a song re-tints everything; covers without colours and the empty state keep the system look and coral. Saved is the sidebar: a column a shade darker than the mesh (black at 22%), or, slid over Practice, the cover's dark shade at 86% over a blur, with a small "Saved" header carrying Sort and Add as bare glyphs, then a pill search field as the list's first row, so it scrolls away with the songs as iOS's does (it keeps fixed AppKit colours, since a list row turned `.secondary` a different grey), the sort's sections under headings as small and quiet as it, and 36pt rows with the saved speed as quiet trailing text. The loaded song gets a speaker glyph, not a highlight, for the same reason as on iOS. Hover is the only fill a row ever gets. The mesh eases from one cover's colours into the next.

The toolbar has no title, since Practice's song title is the title. Change Song is a labelled glass capsule ("change" and "add" are different actions, so the label stays), the mini player is a glass icon at the trailing end, and the sidebar toggle joins them when the sidebar is collapsed. Below about 1180pt the sidebar slides over Practice with a 32% dim instead of squeezing it; the dim closes it.

A Mac sheet says what it will do: "Choose Song" with Open from Change Song, "Add Song to Saved" with Add from the sidebar's +. A click picks a row (a quiet fill, not the accent), Return or a double-click confirms. Browse stays above "Practising lately", as on iOS.

Marking on the Mac is the same quick Mark, with a bubble over the new dot and a halo round it in place of the toast: "Marker 7 · 2:20.1" and three small buttons that show their keys, Name ↩, Edit… ⌥↩ and Undo ⌘Z, so a mouse can click them and the keys are learnt in passing. It lasts six seconds, counted again once the pointer leaves it. Mark is a split button: the main part marks, and a chevron offers Mark and Edit…, which in the menu bar alone went unfound. Return renames the just-marked point, or else whatever the playhead is in, a loop or a point's section, whichever started last, in the lane itself, with five guessed names listed under the field (↑/↓ choose, Return keeps the highlighted one or what's typed, a click keeps a row; Esc keeps the old name). An unnamed "Marker 7" opens with the first row highlighted. The new dot has a double halo and a full-bright label, and Mark stays lit while the bubble shows.

The marker and loop sheets (Mark and Edit… with ⇧M, or Edit Marker Times… with ⌥Return) are 540pt dialogs, not iOS's form: the title centred at the top, Delete… at the bottom left and Cancel and Save (Add for a new marker) at the bottom right, like the song picker. A marker's sheet is Edit Marker: the name with guessed-name chips under it, then the zoomable strip, where ⌥ while dragging a handle gives the finest speed in place of straying off the strip. The times are plain fields in the rounded digits, with no box, in panels with a smaller corner than iOS's; ↑/↓ nudge the focused one by a tenth, ⇧ by a second, said in a tooltip. Under them the nudge row and a line saying where the marker's section runs until ("Runs until Verse 2 at 1:48"), with a small Make a loop at its end, which saves the marker and opens New Loop over its section, already looping. Then Edit Loop's row without Loop: Play from marker (from exactly the marker) and Play into marker (Preview's lead-in), a hairline, then back 5, play and forward 5. Preview alone couldn't tell you whether the marker sat on the beat. A loop's sheet, Edit Loop, has Start and End panels, the nudge row, "36s long · inside Chorus" and one row of bordered buttons: Loop, a hairline, the cues, a hairline, then back 5, play and forward 5. Its Loop loops the loop as edited, its bounds following every nudge and drag, over whatever Practice was looping, which comes back when it's turned off or the sheet closes; solid coral with dark ink when on. The iOS sheet has the same controls, with Loop first. Both platforms share the zoomed strip, the plain marker time and the chips; the Mac's default button isn't solid coral.

Practice on the Mac is a horizontal layout with no panel, divider or card behind anything. The cover (172pt, 112 compact) sits beside the title at 34pt bold (24 compact), with "Artist — Album" under it and a glass "Save at 70%" prompt in a slot held at its height, so the title never moves when it appears. Under the cover, left-aligned, is the transport: restart, back 10, play/pause (the one solid text-colour fill with dark ink, and the largest), forward 10, a short hairline and Loop, solid only when on. The knob (284pt, 216 compact) is centred against that column, with the system volume slider under it at three quarters of its width, left out altogether on an output with no software volume. Below, full width, a header row says what's looping ("Looping Chorus" with its range and length in secondary text, or a dimmed "Loop off") with Mark as a glass capsule at the trailing end, then the timeline with its times under it. Spare height goes above and below the whole stack. Wide or compact is decided from Practice's own size: 900 by 560 and up is wide.

The timeline's marker lane sits above the track, with no panel behind it. Markers get a thin row of their own (a dot with its name beside it); loops are 24pt bars below, a 7pt corner, the name inside: idle is a faint fill with a hairline edge, looping is the solid text-colour fill with dark ink, as Play and Loop are. Overlapping loops stack into extra rows like calendar events, to three, then share the last; a stacked loop gets a ring in the background colour. A name shows only if the whole of it fits (a loop's bar, or the room before the next marker); otherwise it's a bare bar or dot and the tooltip says it, never an ellipsis. Rows are worked out from the lane's width as it resizes, and an empty lane holds one row's height with a dashed hint, so loading a song never moves Practice. There is no pill row: click a loop to add it to or take it out of the chain (as a pill tap does on iOS; taking the last out widens to the whole song), ⌘-click to loop just it, click a marker to jump to it, and with the loop off every click just jumps, since only the Loop button turns looping on. The loop label in the header is a menu of Whole song, every loop (checked when looping, with its range and 1-9 position) and every marker, for what the lane can't show. Under the track, elapsed time sits on the left and the remaining time, with a true minus, on the right.

Hovering a lane item lifts a loop 2pt with a brighter fill and a soft shadow, and raises a dark, hairlined bubble over the lane at once ("**Chorus** · 1:02 – 1:36 · click to loop · double-click to edit", or "click to jump" with the loop off). Double-click opens the item's sheet, as in Finder, where Return renames; it used to rename, which left the sheet reachable only from the right-click menu. It replaces the system tooltip, which waits and would clash with it. Over the track, a small bubble gives the time under the pointer. Right-click opens SwiftUI's own context menu, with only Delete's ⌫ as a hint (the bubble already says what a click does): Loop Just This and Add or Remove from Loop are greyed with the loop off, then Jump to Start, Rename, Edit Times… and Delete. Names are typed in place in a 26pt dark field with a ring in the text colour, not the accent, and "Return to keep · Esc keeps <name>" beside it, where Esc leaves the old name rather than deleting the marker; clicking away discards a new loop. A marker still called "Marker 7" or "Loop 2" opens with the first suggestion highlighted, so Return, Return takes the guess, and the marker's own label steps aside while its field is open.

Dragging on empty lane (the dashed "No markers yet" area too) makes a loop: a dashed ghost bar in the loop row with a dark bubble above it reading "1:40 – 2:04 · 0:24", then, on release, the name field under it; Return keeps it (a blank name becomes "Loop 3", as on iOS), Escape or clicking away discards it. Dragging never starts playback or touches the loop. Times round to whole seconds, tenths with ⌥, and an edge within 6pt of another marker's edge or the song's ends snaps to it. A drag under 4pt, or under the shortest loop, is a click and makes nothing. Within 5pt of a loop's edge the pointer becomes the left-right resize arrow and a white handle, taller than the bar and ringed in the ground colour, appears; dragging it shows "Start 2:20.0 · 0:40" (the edge in tenths, the length) above it, snapping the same way and held inside the song and short of the other edge by the shortest loop. Nothing is saved until release. A loop that is looping keeps playing while it's trimmed: its bounds in the loop follow the drag without a seek, and a playhead left outside it simply wraps as at the end of any lap. The body of a loop can't be dragged, so a click there still loops it.

Zoom is offered only when it's needed: when the whole-song lane would hide more than three names or needs three loop rows, and for as long as the view is zoomed. Then a quiet − / Fit / + glass capsule joins Mark in the header, and a 14pt overview strip sits above the lane: the whole song drawn small like the iOS lane (dots for markers, short bars for loops, the looping one solid) with a hairline box over the part in view. Drag the box to pan, drag its edges to zoom, click outside it to centre the view there. Pinch zooms around the pointer, a two-finger horizontal swipe pans (only while zoomed; vertical scrolls pass through), and zoom stops with about 8 seconds in view. The lane, the track, its played fill, hover time, scrubbing, drag-to-mark, trim and snapping all use the visible range; the times under the track stay the song's. The lane's height is always the whole song's, so zooming never moves Practice; only the strip appearing or disappearing does, by its own height, with a short ease and not on first load. The view pages once when the playhead leaves it (the playhead lands a tenth of the view in from the left), rather than scrolling along, and leaves a playhead alone that the user has panned away from. Choosing a loop that isn't fully in view pans to it, or zooms out to hold it, with a margin. Zoom resets to fit when the song changes and is never saved.

Shortcuts are in tooltips and the File, Playback, Markers and Speed menus, never on screen. Save (⌘S) is in File and says what it saves: Save Speed, Save Loop, or Save Loop and Speed. A walks A·B through Set Loop Start and Set Loop End in Markers, B also sets the end (and only that) without a menu item of its own, which would list Set Loop End twice, and Esc is Discard A–B Loop. Space, the four arrows (←/→ seek 10 seconds, ↑/↓ change the speed by 5%, ⌥↑/⌥↓ by 1%), L, S (Set Speed…, which opens the readout for typing) and 1-9 (the loops in Markers ▸ Saved Loops, listing only those that exist), A and Esc are plain menu key equivalents, with M, ⇧M, Return and ⌥Return in the Markers menu after Playback (Mark, Mark and Edit…, Rename Marker…, Edit Marker Times…) and the Saved Loops submenu after them, handed instead to a text field while one is being edited, Return too (except in a sheet), so they type normally in the search, the name fields and the readout; `TextFieldFocus` records why. The menus grey out while a sheet is up.

The knob turns round with the pointer, as it does under a finger on iOS, or with a two-finger scroll; ⇧ snaps either to the 5% grid, where the pointer then jumps stop to stop. The readout becomes a plain text field in the same rounded bold digits as soon as it's clicked, with no box or border, and grows as it's typed into; a click elsewhere points it, with a faint pointer following the mouse to show where. The scale is the coarse zone, landing on the nearest 5% (or the saved dot, right on it) even where a small knob draws only every 10; the skirt is the fine one, landing on the whole percent under the pointer, or with ⇧ the nearest 5%. A finger keeps ticks-only taps and double-tap, since it covers several percent. On an iPad, a mouse, trackpad or Pencil clicks as the Mac does, with the same hover pointer, click-to-type, Set Speed… and ⇧ snapping, but no right-click menu: on iPadOS that menu also opens on the long press that starts a turn. The knob has no tooltip on the Mac: the hover pointer and the readout's click say how it works. The Mac has no double-click for the saved or full speed, since its first click would already have pointed the knob or opened the readout, and waiting it out made the single click look as if it had done nothing; ↺ and the menus reach both. Right-clicking the knob or the mini player's chip gives Type Speed…, every 5% from 50 to 100 and the saved speed, the same as the Speed menu; the menu bar panel keeps its five, which is all its segmented row has room for. Speed is a menu bar menu rather than a toolbar control: the knob is already on screen beside it.

The mini player is a 452 by 120 card that is its own window: no title bar or chrome, not resizable, floating above other apps' windows, painted with the same mesh and dragged by its background. The cover (96pt) sits inset on the left; beside it, the title with what's looping under it ("Looping Chorus · 0:34 ⌄", or a dimmed "Loop off"), which opens the timeline header's marker menu, since without it the card had no way to see the loops by name, chain them or reach a marker; and at the trailing end the speed as a glass chip in SF Pro Rounded, which takes the knob's drag, scroll and ↑/↓; a click opens Practice's knob (200pt) on the same mesh in a popover below it, since a click that did nothing read as broken. Below: previous loop, play/pause (solid text colour, dark ink), next loop, then Loop (solid when on) and an expand button as glass, and a 4pt progress bar with the chain's loops as a lighter band under the played fill. Previous and next follow the lane's click rule (looping: loop that one alone; off: jump to its start) and are dimmed when there's no loop that way. ⌥⌘M swaps it with the main window, which is hidden rather than closed so Practice keeps its cover colours.

The menu bar extra is the Tempo Ring: a faint full ring, a lit arc from the lower left up round to the right (three quarters of the sweep, as on the canvas), and a centre dot, drawn in SwiftUI and handed to the bar as a template image so it takes the bar's colour. Its 300pt panel is on the mesh: song row with play/pause, the five speeds (50/60/75/90/100) as a segmented row with the current one solid, then the loops as rows with a short bar glyph, name and start time, the looping one solid. With no song it says "No song loaded" and offers a glass "Open Music Momentum".

## Volume

Practice's volume is the system's, the level the side buttons set: Apple's own `MPVolumeView` under the transport in every layout, its native slider and thumb, tinted the text colour, not coral, since volume isn't a state; the transport sits midway between it and the scrubber, so the two bars frame it as a pair. Stacked, it's the first thing to go when the screen runs short of height, before the controls shrink: the side buttons do the same job, and the loops don't.

Wide, the cover sits beside the title as it does stacked, the pair centred over the wheel with the save prompt and Change Song centred on a row of their own under it: in a column that narrow, the prompt growing over the title left only its first word. The playback column holds nothing but playback, and spreads, within limits, to run from the header's top to the wheel's foot, but never closer than 16pt to the top of a landscape phone or its tab bar. Held short by the tab bar, it still starts level with the header and stops short at the bottom; only when that leaves too little room for its rows is it centred on the wheel's column instead. It sits on a glass panel, so the screen is two objects, the knob and the panel, under the title; bare, its rows floated apart and the volume thumb was the heaviest thing on screen. Inside the panel, pills and Mark are a flat fill and a hairline rather than glass on glass. The transport is held to 360pt there: spread across the column, its buttons were 190pt apart and read as a toolbar.

## Type

- **App:** system type. SF Pro Rounded bold for the speed readout, `.monospacedDigit()` for anything that ticks, footnote medium for pills and chips. No custom fonts.
- **Website:** Bricolage Grotesque (700/800) for headings, Instrument Sans for body text, JetBrains Mono for eyebrows, numbers and placeholders.

## Contrast

- Coral is about 7:1 on black but 2.9:1 on white, so coral *text* uses `AccentText`, which darkens to about 5.3:1 in light mode. Fills keep the bright coral.
- White on coral is 2.9:1, too faint for small text. Text on solid coral uses `OnAccent` (about 6.5:1). On an artwork-coloured screen the same job falls to a darker shade of the ground.
- Touch targets are at least 44pt.

## Tried and dropped

- **Combining lists on iPhone and iPad,** with a filter menu beside the chips holding Combine Filters and Match All / Match Any: it repeated every chip, and a two-list intersection is a question for organising, not for picking the next song to practise. It stays on the Mac, which has room to show what a combination means.
- **Clipping or fading the glass list chips beside the card:** glass under any clipping layer, the scroll view's own or the edge fade's mask, frosts that whole layer, so the pills sat in a grey rectangle over the glow. Beside the card the strip is unclipped, and chips scrolled past an end slide under the card or off the window.
- **Combining lists in the Mac sidebar,** with a row of coral removable tags under the search field: the header already named the scope, coral tags read loud over the sidebar's dark mesh, and combining belongs in the Manage window.
- **An orange swipe to remove a song from a list:** orange beside coral reads as the accent. It's grey.
- **A third A·B press discarding the loop, on the Mac:** one press too many threw away a loop caught on the beat, and the Markers menu showed Discard A–B Loop twice. A third press starts a new loop instead; Discard is its own item. iPhone and iPad went back to clearing, since their Discard was hidden in a long-press menu.
- **Mark pausing the song and opening the marker sheet:** it stopped the playing-along Mark is for. Mark saves in place now, and Mark and Edit… is the old path.
- **Edit… in the toast's header, beside Undo:** it was moved there because the chips scrolled it out of sight on a phone. Once the chips wrapped, it went back to being the last chip, where the design board puts it.
- **The toast above the scrubber:** it covered the speed wheel's readout and stopped the knob being dragged. It sits over the pills at the bottom instead.
- **A Point | Clip switch in the marker sheet:** turning a marker into a loop in place deleted the section it started. Make a loop keeps the marker and adds a loop, except for one that isn't a section start yet: a new marker, or the one Mark just made.
- **Make a Point on a loop:** the same loss the other way round, so a loop can't be turned back into a marker.
- **Amber for markers:** read as out of place beside coral.
- **A solid coral confirm button:** beside tinted chips it read as a different, brighter coral. Sheets use a plain toolbar tick in `AccentColor` instead.
- **The cover drawn into Practice's background,** faded from the top: no fade looked right across every cover.
- **The single colour MusicKit ships with artwork** as the ground: it's often the cover's border, not its average.
- **Coral or grey chips beside the floating card:** coral clashed with the cover; grey read as disabled. Hence glass.
- **The floating card on a landscape iPhone Air or Pro Max,** which are just wide enough for Saved beside it: the card was under 400pt tall and Practice scrolled. A phone on its side keeps the tabs and Practice's two-column layout.
- **Highlighting the loaded song in Saved** with a panel, coral or the cover's colour.
- **A section per artist in Saved:** most artists have one saved song, so nearly every row had a header over it. Artist files under letters like Title.
- **Grouping Saved by how full the sections would be,** rather than by song count: switching sort could take the headers away, which read as a bug. Letters for Artist made a plain count enough.
- **Controls on every Saved row:** a glass speed chip and a Mark button down the trailing edge, and the song's marker pills under it (glass, then a hairline). The chips outweighed the titles, and songs with markers were twice as tall as those without. The rows are as plain as the Mac sidebar's now, with the rest behind a long press; markers are edited on Practice.
- **A Mark pill pinned to the end of Practice's clip row:** it took a pill's width, leaving room for two and a half clips on a phone. Mark is a link on the caption line under the row instead.
- **The save and change-song chips beside a short window's wheel,** with a one-line title above, to give the wheel the header's height: mocked up, they didn't sit right beside the dial.
- **Mark in the compact header beside Change song:** the top corner is the hardest reach for a thumb, and Mark is pressed on the beat, mid-song. Compact puts it at the end of the clip row instead.
- **A speed wheel that spun past its limits,** with an arc around the rim reporting the value and a plate, teeth and hub in three greys: the greys muddied each other on a dark cover, and the saved speed appeared nowhere on it. It's an amp knob now: it points at the speed and stops dead at each end.
- **A glass "↺ 80%" chip in the speed knob's bottom gap** to lead back to the saved speed: it read as a control hanging off the knob, and on the small knob, whose ring is shallower than the chip, its glass merged into the skirt's rim. The way back is a bare ↺ under the readout instead; the saved dot already says where it goes.
- **Grey save and change-song chips under the title:** they read as disabled, and "Saved at 70%" repeated the number the knob shows. The saved speed is a dot on the knob's scale instead.
- **A coral-tinted save prompt** on glass tinted to match: beside the neutral Change Song it looked like the two buttons came from different screens.
- **A glass bookmark once the speed was saved:** beside Change Song it looked just as tappable, though tapping it did nothing.
- **A bare bookmark once the speed was saved:** beside the glass Change Song it looked like a button that had lost its glass, and the knob's saved dot already said the same thing.
- **A save prompt that pushed the title aside** reflowed it mid-drag, and **one floating over the title** smeared the letters through its glass. The title keeps room for Change Song and fades out under the prompt.
- **Markers drawn on the timeline's track:** a clip at track height read as buffering, and a looping one needed a halo to survive the played fill. They have a lane of their own above it.
- **A playhead line through the marker lane and the track:** on a song without markers it sat high above the track and the bar looked off-centre, and a short tick centred on the track read as a notch; on a song with them it was one more line across the timeline that the end of the fill already says. The end of the fill marks the position.
- **A ⊢ bracket for clips:** it read as a span, but nothing on the timeline looked like it once clips were drawn there as bars. The pills and the lane now use the same shapes.
- **Practice's stack centred on the screen:** the cover's distance from the top changed with every screen height, and slid about 50pt while a floating card was dragged wider. **Pinning the title and the controls to the edges and giving all spare height to the gap under the title** left a tall card lopsided. **Holding the controls to the bottom edge too** made an iPhone Air look bottom-heavy. The title holds the top; spare height is shared above the wheel, below it and under the controls; and only a screen taller than a phone centres the stack.
- **Moving Practice's cover in to 32pt** so its corner would be concentric with the floating card's left a short title wrapping on a 375pt screen. It's 24pt in with a rounder 16pt corner, and every row shares that margin.
- **Tinting the pill of the clip the playhead is in:** a 12% coral fill and a coral glyph were too faint to notice mid-song, and the section name under the timeline already says where you are.
- **A volume slider styled to match the scrubber:** a thin track and a small dot through `MPVolumeView`'s image setters still sat above its speaker icons on a device. It's the native slider now, though its thumb outweighs the scrubber.
- **A white capsule thumb on the scrubber, the size of the native slider's,** so the scrubber and volume read as a pair: a 38pt thumb hid the spot under the finger and made scrubbing less accurate. The track thickens while a finger is down instead.
- **A volume track of our own driving a hidden `MPVolumeView`:** it matched the scrubber exactly, but reading the level needed an audio session of our own, and setting it went through the hidden view's inner slider.
- **An upright volume slider beside the wheel in the wide layout:** it was `MPVolumeView` turned 90°, which Apple doesn't document or do itself.
- **`MPVolumeView`'s slider forced into iOS 26's thumbless style:** its private slider couldn't be dragged back up from zero, and centring it by hand came undone whenever it laid itself out again.
- **The wide header aligned to the column's leading edge,** with the buttons trailing under it: it fought the centred wheel, and Change Song sat apart from the song it changes.
- **Regrouping the wide transport by meaning:** Mark in the transport with the other on-the-beat buttons, and Loop as a chip leading the clip row it scopes. Mocked up, moving both off their familiar spots made no sense.
- **Taller glass pills,** 36–40pt to match Mark: the extra height cost an iPhone SE its full-size wheel.
- **The wide playback column as a tight block** centred beside the header and wheel: it looked stark. Stretched the whole height, an iPad window's rows drifted apart, so each gap grows by at most 28pt.
- **The cover over the playback column in the wide layout,** up to 180pt: it was the only thing the volume row could take height from, and on a landscape phone it shrank to 76pt.
- **⏮ for restart:** a triangle-and-bar beside the skip buttons' circular arrows looked like a different set, and reads as "previous track". Restart uses `gobackward`, the skip arrow without a number.
- **Turning the Mac's speed knob by dragging up or down,** as Logic's knobs do: it moved by the drag's vertical travel, not where the pointer went, so dragging round the knob turned it the wrong way on one side. It turns round with the pointer instead; the mini player's speed chip, which isn't round, still drags up and down.
- **Restyling the Mac's sidebar toggle** to match the toolbar's glass buttons: over a cover the split view's own toggle fills with the cover's pale text colour while the sidebar shows. A darker window appearance didn't change it, and a hand-made replacement looked worse. It stays the system's toggle, as it naturally looks.
- **Single percents on the Mac knob's scale as well as its skirt,** with the ticks pulling clicks within a percent onto them: next to a tick, the percents either side were half as wide to hit. **The scale's old ticks-only rule, with the hover pointer on the skirt alone,** was two unrelated rules in one knob. The scale is the 5% zone and the skirt the 1% zone instead, both under the hover pointer.
- **A resize (↕) pointer over the Mac's speed knob,** to say it drags up and down: it looked out of place over a round control. The pointer stays the arrow and the tooltip says how to turn it.
- **Keeping the loop menu open** to chain several clips in one go: a SwiftUI `Menu` closes on every click, so each clip added from it takes reopening the menu; the lane is the place to chain.
- **The mesh drawn behind the Mac's split view:** the columns cover anything behind them, so the window stayed white. It is the window's own container background instead.
- **A picked song in the Mac sheet filled with the accent or system blue:** coral there meant "on", and blue is a second tint. The pick is a neutral 14% fill.
- **A hand-built right-click menu in the lane** (a drawn panel, then an `NSMenu` popped up by hand with gesture hints like "⌘ Click" down the right): the first needed its own dismissal and keyboard handling, and the second a local event monitor that could act on a stale hover. `.contextMenu` handles right-click and ctrl-click itself, and the bubble already says what a click does.
- **The system `.help` tooltip on lane items:** it waits about a second, and beside the hover bubble the two clashed. The bubble is the only tooltip there.
- **Alignment guides to hang the lane's bubbles above it:** in a `ZStack` they resized the stack, and the whole lane slid down while a bubble showed. The bubbles hang from a zero-sized frame instead.
- **Continuous scrolling to follow the playhead when zoomed:** the lane's names and bars would slide under the eye all song. The view jumps once, a page at a time.
- **Paging a playhead the user has panned away from:** looking ahead at a clip would be undone on the next tick. Only a playhead that was in view and has just left it pages.
- **A lane height that follows the visible rows:** zooming into a quieter part shrank it and moved everything below. It holds the whole song's height.
- **An 18pt overview strip** (the canvas's): 14pt is enough for dots and bars, and costs Practice less when it appears.
- **A title bar over the mini player** (hidden-title-bar style, traffic lights floating on the card): the lights covered the cover's corner, and the card is meant to read as one object. The plain window style has none.
- **Previous clip stepping from the playhead while a clip loops:** from deep inside the looping clip it went back to that same clip, so the button seemed dead. With a clip loop running, "here" is the loop's clip.
- **A fully lit Tempo Ring arc** (lower left all the way to lower right): it read as a closed dial. The canvas's three-quarter arc leaves the ring's faint part showing and suggests a speed.
